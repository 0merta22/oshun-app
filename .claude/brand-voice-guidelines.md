## Executive Summary

Oshun is a vertically integrated beauty marketplace built exclusively for Black and multicultural consumers — named after the Yoruba goddess of beauty, abundance, and water. The brand voice is deeply cultural, mission-driven, and unapologetically specific. Oshun doesn't hedge its audience or soften its purpose. Every piece of content is grounded in a clear conviction: the Black community has been building the beauty industry for decades and deserves a platform that was built for them.

The brand speaks with the confidence of a founder who has lived the problem — direct, data-backed, and personally invested. Oshun doesn't position itself as a neutral service; it positions itself as economic infrastructure for the culture. This makes the voice feel less like a brand talking at customers and more like a community member building something for everyone in the room.

Content should feel bold but earned, specific but never exclusive. The brand uses precision (real numbers, real products, real communities) to build trust, and uses cultural ownership language ("Built by us. For us.") to signal belonging. The goal is always a community that feels seen and a partner that feels chosen — not a transaction that happened to reach the right audience.

### Voice Attributes Detail

#### Attribute 1: Culturally Specific (Confidence: High)
- **What it means**: Every word is written with Black and multicultural consumers at the center. We don't soften our audience description or broaden language to avoid alienating others.
- **How it shows up**: "Built for Black America." "For us." "Products that actually understand your needs." Specific call-outs to hair textures, skin tones, and culturally specific products.
- **What to avoid**: Generic beauty language that could be Ulta or any mainstream platform. Never say "all consumers" or "beauty lovers" in primary messaging.
- **Evidence**: "The beauty marketplace built for Black America." (Pitch Deck, Slide 1); "built exclusively for Black and multicultural consumers" (DM Scripts, multiple); "products, services, and brands that actually understand your needs." (Social Asset 2)

#### Attribute 2: Mission-Driven (Confidence: High)
- **What it means**: Oshun's reason for existing is larger than commerce. We are building economic infrastructure for a community that has been underinvested in despite outsized spending power and cultural influence.
- **How it shows up**: Using the infrastructure framing ("economic infrastructure for the culture"), referencing the $9.4B market and <2% VC funding gap as context for why this exists — not just as market sizing.
- **What to avoid**: Reducing the brand to delivery speed or app features. Content should never feel like it's only about convenience.
- **Evidence**: "We are not just a delivery app. We are economic infrastructure for the culture." (Pitch Deck, Slide 11); "The Black community has been building the beauty industry for decades. Oshun gives them the platform, the logistics, and the marketplace to own it." (Pitch Deck, Slide 11)

#### Attribute 3: Direct and Data-Backed (Confidence: High)
- **What it means**: We don't hedge. We state things clearly with real numbers. Specificity is how we build trust with both consumers and partners.
- **How it shows up**: $9.4B, 9×, 700K+, 45 minutes, 15-minute call, 5–7 day follow-up cadence. All content uses real, specific data points rather than vague claims.
- **What to avoid**: "We have a large audience" → say "700K+ Black residents in the DC metro area." Vague promises. Open-ended calls to action.
- **Evidence**: Pitch deck leads with $9.4B / 9× / <2% / 700K+ throughout; DM Scripts do/don't table explicitly: "Be specific about audience size and subscriber numbers" / "Make vague promises you can't back up at launch"

#### Attribute 4: Empowering, Not Pitying (Confidence: Medium)

- **Evidence**: Pitch Deck Slide 2: "Black consumers are the most powerful force in beauty — and the most underserved."; Social Asset 3: "Built by us. For us."

#### Attribute 5: Founder-Authentic (Confidence: Medium)
- **What it means**: Oshun's voice carries the founder's personal investment and lived experience. This is not a corporate brand — it's a founder who built the full prototype and has firsthand experience of the gap they're solving.

## Messaging Framework (Confidence: High)

### Primary Value Proposition
Oshun is the first vertically integrated beauty marketplace built exclusively for Black and multicultural consumers — delivering products, services, and brands from a community that has always powered the industry.

Variations observed:
- "The beauty marketplace built for Black America." (Pitch Deck, cover slide)
- "One app. Every beauty need. Built for this community." (Pitch Deck, Slide 4)
- "Sephora meets DoorDash, built specifically for us." (DM Scripts, LinkedIn outreach)

### Key Message Pillars

1. **Cultural Ownership**
   - Core idea: This is built by and for the Black and multicultural community — not adapted for it after the fact
   - When to use: Brand identity content, community-facing social, mission statements, investor close
   - Example phrasing: "Built by us. For us." / "Economic infrastructure for the culture." / "The Black community has been building the beauty industry for decades. Oshun gives them the platform to own it."

2. **Convenience That Actually Serves You**
   - Core idea: On-demand delivery from trusted, culturally-specific sources — not generic delivery that happens to carry some relevant products
   - When to use: Consumer-facing product marketing, app launch content, social media
   - Example phrasing: "45 minutes or less." / "From Black-owned shops near you." / "Products that actually understand your needs."

3. **Economic Infrastructure**
   - Core idea: Oshun solves both sides of the gap — for consumers who can't find trusted sources, and for Black-owned businesses that lack digital infrastructure
   - When to use: Investor content, mission-driven posts, business partner outreach
   - Example phrasing: "We are not just a delivery app. We are economic infrastructure for the culture." / "Local Black-owned beauty businesses have no logistics partner, no delivery channel, no way to reach the customer on their phone in under an hour. Oshun solves that."

4. **Curated, Trusted Discovery**
   - Core idea: The Oshun marketplace is specifically curated for this audience — not algorithmic, not generic, not spray-and-pray
   - When to use: Brand partner pitches, subscription box content, marketplace positioning
   - Example phrasing: "Targeted exposure with a built-in warm audience." / "Curated Black-owned and multicultural brands." / "People who are specifically looking for you."

### Competitive Positioning
- vs. DoorDash / Gopuff: They deliver to everyone. We deliver to the community — from the community.
- vs. Ulta / Sephora: They carry some products. We are built for the customer they don't center.
- vs. Amazon: They have scale. We have specificity, trust, and cultural relevance.
- vs. Status Quo: "Local Black-owned beauty businesses have no logistics partner, no delivery channel, no way to reach the customer on their phone in under an hour."
- Positioning shorthand: "Sephora meets DoorDash, built specifically for us."

## Tone-by-Context Matrix (Confidence: Medium)

Voice is constant. Tone flexes by context.

| Context | Formality | Energy | Technical Depth | Key Principle |
|---------|-----------|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| Instagram / TikTok | Low | High | Low | Punchy, cultural ownership, no preamble |
| Instagram DM (brand outreach) | Low-Medium | Medium | Low | Value-first, conversational, specific product reference |
| LinkedIn (B2B outreach) | Medium-High | Medium | Medium | Concise, founder voice, credibility-forward |
| Cold email (brand partners) | Medium | Medium | Medium-High | Scannable bullets, specific data, clear CTA |
| Investor pitch | High | High | High | Data-backed, bold narrative, strategic close |
| Consumer app content | Low-Medium | High | Low | Warm, direct, community-specific |
| Subscription box / Oshun+ | Low-Medium | High | Low | Curated, exclusive, insider warmth |
| Follow-up messages | Low | Medium | Low | Low-pressure, specific ask, keep the door open |

### Context-Specific Guidelines

#### Social Media (Instagram, TikTok)
- **Overall tone**: Bold, culturally owned, punchy — every post should feel like a complete thought
- **Opening approach**: Lead with the identity statement or a short headline. No preamble.
- **Do's**: Short declarative sentences. Community ownership framing. Reference the community directly ("for us," "your beauty").
- **Don'ts**: Corporate brand speak. Generic beauty language. "We're excited to announce."
- **Examples**: "Beauty, delivered." / "Your beauty. Your way." / "Built by us. For us." / "Beauty for us."

#### Instagram DM / Cold Brand Outreach
- **Overall tone**: Conversational, founder-to-founder, specific — the goal is a reply, not a deal
- **Opening approach**: Lead with the brand's name, then Oshun's identity in one sentence. Get to value by sentence 2.
- **Do's**: Reference a specific product. State the benefit clearly. End with a low-commitment ask.
- **Don'ts**: Generic copy-paste. Vague promises. Leading with what you need. Following up more than once on any channel.
- **Example**: "Hey [Brand Name] — I'm the founder of Oshun — a new beauty marketplace built exclusively for Black and multicultural consumers launching in D.C. this summer. Your [specific product] is exactly what our audience is looking for."

#### LinkedIn (B2B Outreach)
- **Overall tone**: Professional but still personal — founder voice, not corporate voice
- **Opening approach**: Connection note under 300 chars, message after accepted. Don't attach the deck unsolicited.
- **Do's**: Reference a specific product. Use the "Sephora meets DoorDash" positioning shorthand. Ask a redirecting question ("Is there a better person...?").
- **Don'ts**: Over-formal corporate tone. Attaching files in the first message. Generic outreach that could apply to any brand.
- **Example**: "Think of it as Sephora meets DoorDash, built specifically for us."

#### Cold Email (Brand Partners)
- **Overall tone**: Detail-oriented but scannable. DMs get permission — email is where you close.
- **Opening approach**: Subject line is everything. Personalize sentence 1 with a specific product reference. Use bullets for the value prop.
- **Do's**: Lead with a personalized observation about their brand. Use the 5-bullet benefit structure. End with "this week or next?"
- **Don'ts**: Open-ended asks. Sending from a Gmail account. Making promises you can't back up at launch.

#### Investor Pitch
- **Overall tone**: Bold, data-backed, strategic, narrative-driven
- **Opening approach**: Start with the market opportunity → the gap → Oshun's uncontested position → close with mission
- **Do's**: Lead every claim with data. Use the infrastructure framing. Don't soften the cultural specificity.
- **Don'ts**: Downplay the cultural mission. Use vague market sizing.
- **Example**: "The Black community — the most influential consumer force in that industry — has been building it for decades without a platform built for them. Oshun changes that."

## Terminology Guide (Confidence: Medium-High)

### Must-Use Terms
| Term | Usage | Instead Of | Example |
|------|-------|------------|---------|
| "Black and multicultural consumers" | Primary audience descriptor in product/partner contexts | "minority consumers," "urban market," "diverse consumers" | "built exclusively for Black and multicultural consumers" |
| "Economic infrastructure for the culture" | Mission/brand positioning statement | "delivery service," "app," "platform for underserved markets" | "We are not just a delivery app. We are economic infrastructure for the culture." |
| "Founding brand partners" | Early partner designation in B2B outreach | "beta users," "early adopters," "pilot brands" | "We're keeping the founding cohort small — 10–15 national brands" |
| "Curated" | Describing marketplace and subscription box content | "selected," "chosen," "listed" | "Curated Black-owned and multicultural brands shipping nationwide" |
| "Community" | Referring to Black and multicultural consumers in cultural/mission content | "users," "customers" (in cultural contexts) | "Supporting Black-owned beauty businesses, one delivery at a time" |

### Preferred Terms
| Term | Usage | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| "Targeted" | Describing specific audience reach vs. generic distribution | "targeted exposure with a built-in warm audience" |
| "Platform" | B2B and investor contexts — signals infrastructure, not just an app | "Oshun beauty platform" |
| "Featured" | Subscription box placements — editorial value, not just a listing | "I'd love to feature [your product] in one of our first boxes" |
| "Vertically integrated" | Describing the full-stack nature of the offering | "a vertically integrated beauty marketplace" |
| "Founding" | Creating urgency and exclusivity for early partners | "founding cohort," "from Day 1," "founding business partners" |

### Avoid These Terms
| Term | Reason | Alternative |
|------|--------|-------------|
| "Minority consumers" | Diminishing framing — defines by absence, not power | "Black and multicultural consumers" |
| "Urban market" | Coded and reductive | "Black beauty market" or specific city reference |
| "Underserved" (standalone) | Passive framing without power context | Pair it: "the most powerful force in beauty — and the most underserved" |
| "Beauty lovers" / "beauty enthusiasts" | Too broad — erases cultural specificity | "Black women," "Black and multicultural consumers" |
| "Diverse audience" as sole descriptor | Vague; Oshun is specific, not broadly "diverse" | "Black and multicultural beauty consumers" |

### Never-Use Terms (Consumer-Facing)
| Term | Reason |
|------|--------|
| "Spray-and-pray" | Internal sales term only — never use in consumer-facing content |
| "Minority-owned" to describe Oshun | Use "Black-founded" or "Black-owned" — more specific and empowering |

## Language That Works (Confidence: Medium)

### Top Phrases
1. **"Economic infrastructure for the culture"** — Context: mission statements, pitch content, brand positioning. Why it works: Reframes Oshun from a consumer app to a systemic solution. Elevates above any direct competitor.
2. **"Built by us. For us."** — Context: social media, brand identity, community-facing content. Why it works: Community ownership without gatekeeping. Works as a complete standalone thought.
3. **"Sephora meets DoorDash, built specifically for us"** — Context: B2B outreach, investor intro, new audience explanations. Why it works: Instant positioning clarity for any new audience.
4. **"[X] is exactly what our audience is looking for"** — Context: brand partner cold outreach. Why it works: Specific, personalized, value-first — signals the founder has done real research.
5. **"The goal is a reply — not a deal"** — Context: internal outreach methodology. Why it works: Keeps outreach low-pressure and milestone-focused.

### Questions That Work (B2B Outreach)
1. **"Would you be open to it — no commitment, just want to share what we're building?"** — Reduces friction for cold DM response
2. **"Is there a better person to connect with on your team, or would you be the right contact?"** — Either gets a warm referral or confirms the right person
3. **"Would [specific day/time] work for a 15-minute intro call?"** — Specific time request outperforms open-ended scheduling

### Objection Handling Patterns
1. **Objection**: "We only work with established platforms"
   **Response pattern**: "That's exactly why I'm reaching out now. Founding brand partners get placement, attention, and analytics that later entrants won't."
   **Why it works**: Reframes the objection as a reason to act now, not a reason to wait.

2. **Objection**: "Our products are already on Amazon / Sephora"
   **Response pattern**: "Amazon and Sephora don't have a curated audience of Black women who specifically chose that platform for their beauty needs."
   **Why it works**: Distinguishes on specificity and purchase intent, not just channel presence.

3. **Objection**: "We don't have the budget for placement fees"
   **Response pattern**: "No problem at all — we have a product donation model where you contribute units at cost in exchange for full placement exposure and analytics."
   **Why it works**: Removes the financial barrier without devaluing the partnership.

4. **Objection**: "We're not sure our target customer uses platforms like this"
   **Response pattern**: "Black women ages 18–45 are the highest-spending beauty consumers in the U.S. and they over-index on mobile commerce. Our platform was built specifically for them."
   **Why it works**: Backs the claim with data and reframes around audience intent.

## Language to Avoid

### Anti-Patterns
1. **Generic beauty brand language** ("your glow," "beauty for everyone") — Problem: erases cultural specificity. Better: Lead with community identity.
2. **Passive community framing** ("finally someone is serving this community") — Problem: positions community as waiting to be helped. Better: Acknowledge community power first.
3. **Vague promises** ("we have a growing audience") — Problem: destroys credibility with brand partners. Better: Use real numbers even if small.
4. **Open-ended CTAs** ("let me know when you're free") — Problem: low response rates. Better: Propose a specific time.
5. **Leading with what you need** ("I'd love a product donation") — Problem: puts the ask before the value. Better: Lead with what they get.
6. **"We're excited to announce"** — Problem: corporate filler. Better: State the thing directly.
